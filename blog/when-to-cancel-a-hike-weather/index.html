When to Cancel a Hike: Reading the Weather Before You Leave

Practical Hong Kong hiking routes, gear advice and trail planning tips.

Home / When to Cancel a Hike: Reading the Weather Before You Leave

Most bad days on Hong Kong trails start with a forecast nobody read carefully. This is the short list of signals that should change your plan, in the order they appear on the Hong Kong Observatory site.

Signals that mean cancel, not postpone

  • Tropical Cyclone Warning Signal 8 or above. Trails close, transport stops, and the parks are legally off-limits in practice. Do not go.
  • Amber or Red Rainstorm Warning. Stone steps turn into slides within minutes, and valley paths flood. Even a grade 2 route becomes dangerous.
  • Thunderstorm Warning. This is the one people ignore. Lightning on an exposed ridge is a genuine hazard, and Sai Kung and Lantau ridges have no shelter.

Signals that mean adjust, not cancel

  • Very Hot Weather Warning. Shorten the route, start before 8am, double your water, and drop one grade in ambition.
  • Strong Monsoon Signal. Fine on sheltered valley paths; unpleasant and occasionally dangerous on ridges and summits.
  • Visibility below about 5 km in the urban area. Usually means cloud on anything above 500 metres. Choose a low route or accept a white-out.

Then check it again at the trailhead

A forecast read the night before is not a forecast. Weather here changes within hours, particularly between May and September. Check the radar image on the morning you leave, and look west — most summer storms arrive from that direction.

If you are already out and the sky turns dark, leave the ridge immediately rather than trying to finish. Descending in rain is uncomfortable; being caught on a summit in a thunderstorm is the situation rescue teams are called for.
