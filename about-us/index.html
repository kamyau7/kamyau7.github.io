About Us

Ridge & Coast is an independent guide to hiking in Hong Kong, published by a small team who walk these trails in their own time and write down what they find — including the parts that are unpleasant.

Why we started

Hong Kong has more than 300 kilometres of marked trail inside its country parks, and almost all of it is reachable by public transport. Yet the information available to a first-time hiker is thin in a specific way: the official notices tell you a path exists and is closed for maintenance, and social media tells you a viewpoint is beautiful, but neither tells you that the last 400 metres is a scramble, that the only water tap is at the halfway point, or that the bus you planned to catch back stops running at seven.

We write the page we wished existed before our first hike on Lantau: distance, climb, surface, water, toilets, mobile signal, and a plain answer to "can my friend who does not exercise do this?" Content on this site is written for the person standing at the trailhead at 8am deciding whether to turn around.

How every guide is built

Each trail page follows the same six checks before it is published. This is deliberate — consistency is how a reader learns to trust a rating.

  1. Walk it. Every route on the site has been completed end to end by at least one member of the team, in the direction we describe.
  2. Cross-check the data. Distance, ascent and trailhead access are compared against the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department (AFCD) country park information and the official MacLehose Trail notes.
  3. Check transport twice. Bus and minibus routes are re-checked against the operator's current timetable, including the last service of the day.
  4. Photograph the awkward bits. The junction that is easy to miss, the sign that is wrong, the section with no shade.
  5. Write the downgrade. If a route is harder than its reputation, we say so in the opening paragraph instead of hiding it at the bottom.
  6. Re-check every season. Pages carry a last-updated date. If a trail closes for fire prevention or typhoon damage, the page is updated before the next weekend.

Our difficulty scale

Hong Kong does not have an official hiking grade, so we use a five-point scale based on the combination of distance, total climb, surface quality and exposure. The grade assumes a dry day. Add one point in heavy rain or in July heat.

Editorial standards

  • No paid rankings. No gear brand or tour operator has ever paid to appear, or to be ranked higher, on this site.
  • Corrections are public. When a route changes or a fact is wrong, we fix the page and note the change at the bottom rather than quietly editing it.
  • Dates are real. Every guide shows when it was last checked. An old date means the page has not been re-walked recently, and you should treat the details accordingly.
  • We do not replace official advice. For closures, fire risk and weather warnings, the AFCD and Hong Kong Observatory notices always override anything written here.
  • Safety first, aesthetics second. If a route is dangerous in the wrong conditions, the page says so at the top — not in a footnote.

Spotted something out of date on a trail page? Tell us here and we will re-check it. Corrections from readers are how most of our water-point and transport details get fixed.
