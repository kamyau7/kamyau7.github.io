Best Hiking Trails in Hong Kong: 8 Routes to Explore

Eight routes that cover the range of what Hong Kong hiking offers: a city-edge ridge, a world-famous coastal stage, a mountain of silver grass, a scramble, and four quieter alternatives. Each entry gives distance, climb, realistic time, transport and the season it is actually good in.

Grades follow our five-point scale. Times assume an unhurried pace with short breaks, and a dry day. Every route below has been walked end to end by our team.

Best Hiking Trails in Hong Kong: All Eight Routes at a Glance

Hong Kong Hiking Trail Difficulty Ratings

Ranking is not purely by difficulty. We weight five things: how distinctive the scenery is, whether the route works without a car, how much of it is exposed to sun or road noise, whether the surface is kind to knees, and how crowded it gets on a Sunday. A short trail with one exceptional hour can rank above a long one with none.

If a route has a reputation bigger than its reality, we say so here. Dragon's Back is the most famous hike in Hong Kong and also the most overrated by people who have not walked it — it is lovely, but the first 40 minutes is a road.

1. Dragon's Back

Dragon's Back, Hong Kong Island

The ridge above Shek O is the closest thing Hong Kong has to a perfect half-day hike: a genuine ridgeline, sea on three sides, and a finish at a surf beach with a bus stop. Time magazine once called it the best urban hike in Asia, and for once the marketing is roughly accurate — with one caveat most guides skip.

The route starts with about forty minutes of paved road climbing from To Tei Wan to the ridge proper. It is shaded, it is dull, and it is the reason people quit early. Push through: the ridge that follows is open, breezy and genuinely spectacular, following the spine of the hill above Big Wave Bay with the South China Sea to your right.

The descent is where care matters. The path drops steeply through scrub to Big Wave Bay, and the stone steps are polished by traffic and slick after rain. Trekking poles help. At the bottom there is a beach, a couple of cafés, and bus 9 back to Shau Kei Wan MTR.

  • Distance: 8.5 km one way
  • Ascent: roughly 250 m
  • Time: 3–4 hours
  • Grade: 2 / 5
  • Getting there: MTR to Shau Kei Wan, then bus 9 towards Shek O; alight at To Tei Wan
  • Best time: October to March, weekdays before 10am

2. MacLehose Trail Stage 2

MacLehose Trail Stage 2, Sai Kung

If you only do one full day of hiking in Hong Kong, this is the one. Stage 2 runs from Long Ke across the Sai Kung East Country Park to Pak Tam Au, passing four beaches that would each be a destination on their own: Sai Wan, Ham Tin, Tai Wan and Chek Keng.

The climb over Sai Wan Shan is the hard part — a sustained set of steps in full sun with no shade and no water. Do it in the morning. After that the route settles into a rhythm of beach, headland, beach, and the temptation to stop for a swim at every one is the main reason people finish late.

Two notes that matter. First, the beach villages sell drinks and noodles at weekends, which makes this the one route here where you can travel slightly lighter — but do not depend on them on a weekday. Second, the exit at Pak Tam Au is served by a bus that runs frequently enough to make this a reasonable car-free day.

  • Distance: 13.5 km
  • Ascent: roughly 450 m
  • Time: 5–6 hours
  • Grade: 3 / 5
  • Getting there: bus or minibus from Sai Kung town to Sai Wan Pavilion, then walk 30 minutes to the trailhead
  • Best time: November to March; start by 9am

3. Sunset Peak

Sunset Peak (Tai Tung Shan), Lantau

Sunset Peak is 869 metres, the third-highest point in Hong Kong, and for six weeks a year — usually late November into early January — its slopes turn gold with silver grass. That is the version of the mountain you have seen in photographs. The other eleven months it is a long, exposed staircase with a view of the airport.

The route from Pak Kung Au climbs steadily for about 500 metres through forest before breaking onto open hillside. Above the treeline, the old stone huts of the missionary retreat at Nam Shan look like a film set, and the final push to the summit is a stair climb with no protection from wind.

Descending towards Mui Wo, or returning the way you came, both work. Do not plan the summit for sunset unless you have a head torch and are confident on steps in the dark — the descent takes well over an hour and the popular "sunset hike" is the source of more late-night callouts on Lantau than any other route.

  • Distance: 9 km return
  • Ascent: roughly 650 m
  • Time: 4–5 hours
  • Grade: 4 / 5
  • Getting there: bus from Tung Chung or Mui Wo to Pak Kung Au
  • Best time: November to January for the grass; avoid in high wind

4. Sharp Peak

Sharp Peak (Nam She Tsim), Sai Kung

Sharp Peak is the hardest day hike on this list and the one that most deserves its reputation. At 468 metres it is not especially high, but it rises straight out of the coast above Tai Long Wan with loose, sandy scrambles on both approach and descent. The local name, "the first of Hong Kong's three sharp peaks", is earned.

The standard loop starts at Pak Tam Au, follows the coast path past Chek Keng, then climbs the ridge to the summit before dropping to Ham Tin and returning by the Stage 2 path. It is long — around 14 kilometres with 700 metres of climbing — and parts of the scramble require hands on rock.

Do not attempt this in wet weather, in summer heat, or as a first hike. Do not attempt it alone. The exposure on the summit ridge is real, and the descent path is easy to lose in mist. If you want the views without the risk, the beach path below gives you most of the scenery.

  • Distance: 14 km loop
  • Ascent: roughly 700 m
  • Time: 6–7 hours
  • Grade: 5 / 5
  • Getting there: bus from Sai Kung to Pak Tam Au
  • Best time: November to February, dry conditions only

5. Lion Rock

Lion Rock, Kowloon

Lion Rock is the symbol on Hong Kong's collective imagination — the "under the lion rock" spirit of a city that improvised its way forward. The hike itself is a short, steep climb from the edge of Kowloon to a 495-metre summit with one of the best city views in Asia.

Start from Wong Tai Sin or Lok Fu and climb through Lion Rock Park. The path is steps almost all the way, which makes it harder than its 5.5 kilometres suggests: about 400 metres of ascent in under three kilometres. The reward is the view straight down onto the Kowloon grid and across the harbour.

The final section to the lion's head involves a short, exposed scramble with a chain. It is manageable for most people in dry weather, but there is a genuine drop on one side and it is not a place for children or anyone uneasy with heights. The summit plateau just below it is a fine alternative viewpoint.

  • Distance: 5.5 km
  • Ascent: roughly 400 m
  • Time: 2.5–3 hours
  • Grade: 3 / 5
  • Getting there: MTR to Wong Tai Sin or Lok Fu, then walk to Lion Rock Park
  • Best time: October to March; late afternoon for the light

6. Victoria Peak loop

Victoria Peak loop, Hong Kong Island

The Peak circular walk is the best grade 1 hike in the city and the one to do on the day you arrive. It is fully paved, shaded for most of its length, and loops around the summit with views over the harbour on one side and the southern islands on the other.

Most visitors ride the Peak Tram up, walk the flat loop, and ride down. A better version: walk up from Central via the Old Peak Road, which adds about an hour of steep paved climbing and gives you the satisfaction of having earned the view. Either way, go early — by mid-morning the loop is busy with tour groups.

Facilities are unusually good here: toilets, cafés and a shopping centre at the top. This is also the only route on the list that is genuinely pleasant in summer, because the altitude and the tree cover keep it several degrees cooler than the city below.

  • Distance: 3.5 km loop
  • Ascent: roughly 80 m on the loop
  • Time: 1–1.5 hours
  • Grade: 1 / 5
  • Getting there: Peak Tram from Central, bus 15, or walk up Old Peak Road
  • Best time: any month; before 9am to avoid crowds

7. Tai Mo Shan

Tai Mo Shan, New Territories

At 957 metres, Tai Mo Shan is the highest point in Hong Kong, and the walk up it is less dramatic than you would hope — much of the upper route follows a paved service road to the weather radar station. What it lacks in wildness it makes up for in scale: on a clear winter day you can see the whole New Territories laid out below you.

The best approach is from Tai Mo Shan Country Park car park via the nature trail, which climbs through forest and then open grassland. Above 700 metres the vegetation thins and the wind picks up sharply — this is the coldest, windiest place on the list, and people underestimate it every winter.

Because it is the highest point, cloud sits on the summit more often than not. Check the visibility from below before committing; a hazy day at sea level is usually a white-out up top.

  • Distance: 10 km return
  • Ascent: roughly 600 m
  • Time: 4–5 hours
  • Grade: 3 / 5
  • Getting there: bus or minibus to Tsuen Wan, then taxi or bus towards Route Twisk
  • Best time: November to February, on a clear day

8. Cape D'Aguilar

Cape D'Aguilar, Hong Kong Island

The southeast tip of Hong Kong Island is the quietest coastline within reach of the city, and the walk to it passes two things you will not find elsewhere on this list: a sea cave, and a 19th-century lighthouse on a wave-cut platform of black volcanic rock.

The route follows a paved access road from near Shek O, then descends to the cape on a rough path. It is mostly flat, which makes it a good option when you want distance without climbing — and a good option for a group of mixed fitness.

It is exposed, though. There is very little shade and no water on the way, and the wind at the cape can be strong enough to make the rocks slippery with spray. Bring a hat and go in the cooler months.

  • Distance: 8 km return
  • Ascent: roughly 200 m
  • Time: 3 hours
  • Grade: 2 / 5
  • Getting there: MTR to Shau Kei Wan, then bus 9 towards Shek O
  • Best time: October to April, on a clear day

Which Hong Kong Hiking Trail Should You Choose?

  • First hike in Hong Kong: Victoria Peak loop, then Dragon's Back.
  • One full day, best scenery: MacLehose Trail Stage 2.
  • Best photographs: Sunset Peak in late November, or Lion Rock at dusk.
  • Hardest thing you can do in a day: Sharp Peak — dry weather, experienced group only.
  • Group with mixed fitness: Cape D'Aguilar, or the Peak loop plus a walk down to Central.
