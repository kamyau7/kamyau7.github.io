Pat Sin Leng Hike: Route and Trail Guide

Plan the Pat Sin Leng ridge hike with route information, terrain advice, practical preparation and tips for choosing suitable weather conditions.

Home / Pat Sin Leng Hike: Route and Trail Guide

Pat Sin Leng is eight peaks in a row along a single grassy ridge above Plover Cove Reservoir, and it is the finest long ridgeline walk in the New Territories. There is almost no shade, no water and no shortcut once you commit — but the sense of walking a mountain range rather than a hill is unmatched anywhere else in Hong Kong.

Each of the eight summits is named after one of the Eight Immortals of Chinese mythology. They are not eight separate mountains so much as one continuous ridge with eight bumps, which means the climbing is relentless but never brutal: roughly 700 metres of ascent spread over nine kilometres of walking.

  • Distance: 9 km one way
  • Ascent: roughly 700 m
  • Time: 5–6 hours
  • Grade: 4 / 5
  • Start: Tai Mei Tuk, then taxi or walk to the trailhead at Bride's Pool Road
  • Finish: Hok Tau or Cloudy Hill Road, with a bus from Fanling

Why this ridge and not another

Most Hong Kong ridges give you sea views. Pat Sin Leng gives you something rarer: a view of the territory's interior. From the middle of the ridge you look south over Plover Cove Reservoir's drowned valleys, north into the Shenzhen skyline, and west along a line of hills that continues unbroken to Tai Mo Shan.

The surface is also kind for a route of this length. It is grass and packed earth rather than stone steps, which is easier on the knees and much faster underfoot. This is a hill walk in the British sense rather than a Hong Kong staircase — but it is fully exposed to sun and wind.

Pat Sin Leng Hiking Route: Section-by-Section Guide

1. The climb to the first peak (60–75 minutes)

From Bride's Pool Road the path climbs through woodland before breaking onto the ridge at Hsien Ku Fung, the first and steepest of the eight. This is the only genuinely shaded section, and the only place you can change your mind without a long retreat.

2. The eight summits (180–210 minutes)

What follows is a rhythm: up a grassy shoulder, along a short crest, down into a saddle, up again. None of the individual climbs is long, but the cumulative effect is real. The high point is Sam Sin Fung at 639 metres, roughly halfway along.

Navigation is straightforward in clear weather — the path follows the ridge line and is well worn. In mist it becomes much harder, because every grassy shoulder looks identical and there are side paths dropping to the villages below. Carry a map if the forecast mentions low cloud.

3. The descent (90–120 minutes)

The final descent off the western end is steep and, in wet weather, slippery with grass and loose soil. It drops towards Hok Tau, where a minibus runs to Fanling. Alternatively a side path leads down to Cloudy Hill Road for a shorter exit.

Pat Sin Leng Hike: Terrain and Practical Challenges

Three things catch people out on Pat Sin Leng, and all three are about planning rather than fitness.

  • Getting to the start. There is no MTR at the trailhead. The usual approach is a taxi from Tai Mei Tuk to Bride's Pool Road, which costs around HK$60–80 and is worth it. Buses are infrequent.
  • Getting away from the finish. Hok Tau has a minibus but it stops early. Check the last departure before you start, or arrange to walk out to Cloudy Hill Road.
  • Water. There is none on the ridge at all. Two litres per person is the minimum in winter, three in spring.

Best Time to Hike Pat Sin Leng

November to February. In summer this ridge is a frying pan: nine kilometres of full sun with no shade and no escape routes, and the grass holds heat close to the ground. October and March are workable if you start early.

Winter also brings the clearest air, which matters here more than on a coastal route — the whole point of the walk is the long view across the New Territories.

Official Pat Sin Leng Hiking Information

The AFCD describes Wilson Trail Stages 9 and 10 through Pat Sin Leng as a challenging 17.4 km route taking about seven hours, and recommends it for hikers confident in their fitness. Read the AFCD Pat Sin Leng Country Park guide for official route alternatives and facilities. Check the Hong Kong Observatory Hiking Trail Weather Service before hiking the exposed ridge.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to walk all eight peaks?

No. The classic full traverse takes 5–6 hours, but you can turn back after three or four summits and return the way you came. That version takes about three hours and still gives you the ridge experience.

Is there any scrambling?

Almost none. This is a walking route on grass and earth. The difficulty comes from length, exposure and the cumulative climbing, not from technical terrain.

Pat Sin Leng or Tai Mo Shan?

Pat Sin Leng for the ridge walk and the views; Tai Mo Shan for the summit and the sense of scale. Pat Sin Leng is the better hike, Tai Mo Shan the easier logistics.
