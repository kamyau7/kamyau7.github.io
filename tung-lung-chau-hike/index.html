Tung Lung Chau Hike: Route and Trail Guide

Tung Lung Chau is the best short hike in Hong Kong that most people have never heard of. Twenty-five minutes by ferry from Sai Wan Ho, the island has cliffs, a prehistoric rock carving, the territory's oldest lighthouse and a loop walk that takes under three hours — with almost nobody else on it.

It is the route to choose when you want the sea rather than a summit, or when you have a morning free and no appetite for 700 metres of stairs. The climbing is modest, the terrain is varied, and the ferry back is the only real scheduling constraint.

  • Distance: 5 km loop
  • Ascent: roughly 200 m
  • Time: 2.5–3 hours
  • Grade: 2 / 5
  • Start and finish: Tung Lung Chau public pier
  • Access: ferry from Sai Wan Ho or Sam Ka Tsuen

Tung Lung Chau: Hiking Highlights and Attractions

Three things justify the ferry fare, and all three are within a short walk of each other.

The rock carving. A 3,000-year-old Bronze Age carving of a dragon or bird, cut into a boulder near the shore and protected as a declared monument. It is smaller than photographs suggest and fenced off, but it is genuinely ancient and genuinely here — one of the oldest human marks in Hong Kong.

The lighthouse. Built in 1875, the Tung Lung Chau light is the oldest surviving lighthouse in Hong Kong. It sits on a headland above a wave-cut platform of dark volcanic rock, with cliffs falling straight into the sea.

The cliffs. The eastern side of the island is a climbing venue — sea cliffs of volcanic tuff used by Hong Kong's rock climbing community. Even if you do not climb, the clifftop path gives you views across the Tathong Channel with container ships passing below.

Tung Lung Chau Hiking Route: Walking the Island Loop

From the pier, follow the signposted path south along the ridge. It climbs gently through grass and scrub to a saddle, where the path splits: left to the rock carving and the campsite, right towards the lighthouse.

Take the right-hand branch first while the day is cool. The trail follows the clifftop, passes above the climbing routes, and descends steeply to the lighthouse on a rough path with some scrambling. Allow time here — the platform around the light is one of the most dramatic spots in Hong Kong, and the final drop to it is not for anyone uneasy with heights.

Return the way you came to the saddle, then take the other branch down to the rock carving and the small campsite, before looping back along the coast to the pier. The whole circuit is about five kilometres, with a couple of steep sections that need hands.

How to Get to Tung Lung Chau by Ferry

This is the one route on the site where scheduling matters more than fitness. Ferries run from Sai Wan Ho and Sam Ka Tsuen, mostly at weekends and public holidays, and they are not frequent. Miss the last one and you are staying on the island — which is possible, since there is a campsite, but not part of your plan.

Check the current timetable before you go, buy a return ticket, and note the departure time in your phone. The crossing takes about 25 minutes from Sai Wan Ho.

What to Bring for a Tung Lung Chau Hike

  • Water and food for the whole trip. There are no shops on the island. None.
  • Shoes with grip for the clifftop descent to the lighthouse.
  • Sunscreen. The whole island is exposed; there is very little tree cover.
  • A windproof layer. The Tathong Channel funnels wind, and the headland is cold in winter.
  • Cash for the ferry — some services do not take cards.

Best Time to Visit Tung Lung Chau

October to April. The island is exposed in every direction, so summer heat is unforgiving and winter wind is bracing but manageable. Because ferries mostly run at weekends, this is one of the few routes where a weekend is the natural choice rather than something to avoid.

Autumn is the best window: the water is still warm enough to swim at the small beach near the pier, and the air is clear enough to see the ships in the channel.

Frequently asked questions

Can I camp on Tung Lung Chau?

Yes. There is an official campsite near the rock carving with basic facilities. It is popular in autumn and needs no booking, but arrive early at weekends.

Is the lighthouse path dangerous?

It is steep and rough in two places, with a short section where you use your hands. In dry weather it is fine for anyone reasonably steady on their feet. In rain or high wind, skip it.

Do I need to book the ferry?

Usually no, but services are limited and popular in fine weather. Arrive at the pier early and check the return times before boarding.
