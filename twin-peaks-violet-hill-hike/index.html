Twin Peaks Hike Hong Kong: Route and Trail Guide

Discover the Twin Peaks hike in Hong Kong, including the Violet Hill and Twins route, steep steps, preparation advice and the best time to hike.

Home / Twin Peaks Hike Hong Kong: Route and Trail Guide

The Twins are the two steep hills above Tai Tam, connected by a staircase so relentless that Hong Kong hikers use them as a fitness test. Add Violet Hill on the front and you have a four-hour route with 700 metres of climbing, a cemetery, a reservoir and a beach bar at the end.

It is short — under eight kilometres — which makes it look easy on paper. It is not easy. Almost every metre of this route is either up or down, mostly on steps, and the two climbs of the Twins come with no warm-up and no mercy.

  • Distance: 7.5 km one way
  • Ascent: roughly 700 m
  • Time: 4–5 hours
  • Grade: 4 / 5
  • Start: Wong Nai Chung Gap, above Happy Valley
  • Finish: Stanley, via the beach at Tai Tam Gap

Twin Peaks Hike: Violet Hill, the Twins and Stanley

Understanding the shape of this route is the difference between enjoying it and suffering through it. It has three distinct climbs, each with its own character.

Violet Hill — 1,100 steps as a warm-up

The route begins at Wong Nai Chung Gap and passes through the Hong Kong Cemetery, which is a strange and quietly beautiful start: colonial headstones, mature trees and long grass on a hillside above the city. Then the steps begin. Violet Hill is the gentler of the three climbs, but it comes first and there is no warm-up, so it feels harder than it is.

The Twins — staircases without relief

After a short saddle the path hits the first Twin: roughly 1,000 stone steps laid almost vertically, with no landing, no switchback and no flat section. At the top you immediately descend 800 steps into the col between the two hills, then climb the second Twin. This is where people stop. It is entirely survivable, but you must pace it — small steps, steady rhythm, no stopping to look up.

Stanley — the reward

From the second Twin a long path descends towards Tai Tam and then down to the coast at Stanley, where there are cafés, a beach and buses back to Central. The last section is shaded and gentle, and it is the only part of the route where you can walk side by side and talk.

Why hikers use this route for training

The Twins are the standard conditioning route for anyone preparing for a longer Hong Kong hike, because they compress a lot of climbing into a short distance with a reliable exit. If you are preparing for Sharp Peak or a full MacLehose section, walking this twice a week for a month will do more for your legs than any amount of flat walking.

That also explains the weekend crowd. This is not a solitary route — it is busy with runners and hikers most of Saturday. Go early, or go on a weekday.

What to Bring for the Twin Peaks Hike

  • Shoes with grip. Wet stone steps make this route genuinely hazardous; the steps here are polished by the traffic they receive.
  • One and a half litres of water. There is no tap until Stanley.
  • Trekking poles if your knees dislike descending — there are 2,000 down-steps on this route.
  • Patience. The Twins are a mental challenge as much as a physical one.

Best Time to Hike Twin Peaks

October to March. The route is largely shaded by scrub at the start, but the Twin staircases are in full sun, and climbing stone steps in July humidity is miserable. A winter morning with a clear forecast is ideal — the views over Tai Tam from the top of the Twins are the best part of the day.

Frequently asked questions

How many steps are on the Twins?

Roughly 1,000 up the first Twin, 800 down into the col, then about 900 up the second. Numbers vary by how you count the landings, but it is over 2,500 steps in total across the route, most of them descending.

Can I do just the Twins without Violet Hill?

Yes. Start at Tai Tam Gap instead, which cuts out Violet Hill and the cemetery and leaves a two-and-a-half-hour route. Transport to Tai Tam Gap is by bus from Central.

Is it suitable for beginners?

The Twins alone, yes, if you are reasonably fit and take it slowly. The full Violet Hill and Twins route, no — the cumulative climbing is too much for a first outing.

Looking for something gentler on Hong Kong Island? The trail index lists Dragon's Back and the Peak loop, both grade 1–2. Read the beginner's guide before your first attempt at the Twins.
