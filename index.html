Hong Kong Hiking Guide exists because most Hong Kong hiking advice is either a list of Instagram viewpoints with no route detail, or an official trail notice written for people who already know the area. This site sits in between: every route gets a grade, a realistic time, a water plan and a way back to the city.

We cover the country parks of the New Territories, the islands and the urban-edge hills of Kowloon and Hong Kong Island. If you have a free Saturday and a pair of shoes with grip, there is a route here for you — and we will tell you honestly when a trail is harder than the internet claims.

Why Hong Kong is an odd, excellent place to hike

Roughly three-quarters of Hong Kong's land is not built up, and about 40 per cent of it sits inside country parks protected by law since 1976. That is why you can leave a dense MTR station at nine in the morning and be on a ridge with no buildings in sight before ten.

The trade-off is scale. Most hills here are 300 to 950 metres rather than alpine, so the difficulty comes from heat, humidity and stairs rather than altitude. A route described as "easy" in a guidebook may still mean 700 metres of stone steps in 32°C with 80 per cent humidity — which is why every entry on this site carries a grade and a season.

Weather moves fast too. A clear morning can turn into a thunderstorm by early afternoon between May and September, and the winter months bring strong, cold wind on exposed ridges. Both are manageable with a plan; neither is manageable if you started at noon with one bottle of water.

If you only read one thing: carry at least 1.5 litres of water per person for any hike over two hours, tell someone your route, and check the Hong Kong Observatory forecast on the morning you leave — not the night before.
